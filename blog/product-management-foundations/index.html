Product Management Foundations: A Working List of Truths

There are very few universal truths in product management. But not none. Here’s a working list I’ve collected from years building, coaching, and shipping, across orgs big and small, with tooling ranging from IoT to GenAI.

  1. 01

    🧭Customers Are the Center of the Universe

    The better a product solves their problem(s), the more profitability opportunity there is. Full stop.

  2. 02

    🪑The Three-Legged Stool: Eng, Product, Design

    These three functions must be in lockstep. They don’t always all sit at the table, but when one leg is missing, product quality wobbles.

    Reality check: I’ve shared designers more often than not. That doesn’t make them any less essential.

  3. 03

    📊Data Tells a Story. Anecdotes Give It Soul.

    When the two align, you have conviction. When they don’t, dig deeper.

  4. 04

    🧠PMs Are Not ER Doctors

    Nothing needs to be decided right now. Urgency ≠ panic.

    Know thyself: I make the best decisions through dialogue (talking things out) or writing. I tap trusted advisors (Eng, Design, PMM) based on the nature of the decision.

  5. 05

    ❌~15–20% of PM Time = Error Case Design

    Error messages. Fallback states. Failure flows. Error UX is real product work. Build for it or your users pay the price.

  6. 06

    🗺️All Roadmap Items Serve One of Three Stakeholder Buckets

    • Engineering: e.g. tech debt, refactors
    • Business: e.g. churn mitigation, monetization
    • Customer: e.g. UX wins, new features

    It’s our job to prioritize across these lenses, not serve any one stakeholder unilaterally.

  7. 07

    🔄A Roadmap Should Be Alive

    Agile ≠ chaos. A quarterly roadmap should include top-line priorities and flex room. If it’s too rigid, it’s not Agile. If it changes every two weeks due to sales, it’s not strategy.

  8. 08

    🍷Drunk Driving Product Development (Avoid It)

    You’ve seen it. Maybe you’re in it:

    “This is the most important launch of the year!” (midway through dev) →
    “Actually, this other thing is priority #1!”
    “Quick, ship it. Done is better than right!”

    Result: tech debt landmines, context loss, and exhausted teams.
    Great PMs defend clarity over chaos.

  9. 09

    🤝Design and Eng Are Brilliant, But Speak Different Languages

    If the customer problem is clear, you can solve it a few different ways.
    PMs are there to bridge:

    • Design’s ideal state
    • Eng’s feasibility
    • Customer’s real needs

    100% of the time, a brainstorm + whiteboard can sort it.

  10. 10

    🚘There’s a Porsche Carrera and a Geo Metro Version of Every Feature

    Login. Error modals. Telemetry. You name it.

    What ships? Usually something in the middle. The PM’s job is to define the right middle: viable, delightful, and on time.

Closing Thought

Product management is pattern recognition at scale. You’re rarely solving something for the first time, but it’s often the first time this team is solving this version of the problem.

Your job? Bring clarity, care, and craft to every step.
